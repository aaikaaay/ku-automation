The Challenge

A $1.25M concept package. One engineering question. Two hours to answer.

A European EPC was reviewing a $1.25M gas-to-power trial package proposed by a process equipment supplier to one of their energy major clients. The concept centred on conditioning natural gas from a wellhead through pressure letdown stages before feeding a power generation unit.

The EPC's engineer had a specific technical concern: "What is the JT effect on pressure drop and resulting temperature decrease? Can you produce a table profile? Or do we need simulation software?"

What looked like a one-line question was actually a pre-FEED independent review request. The concept proposal contained a two-stage pressure letdown from three possible source conditions, two gas compositions, and a downstream trim-heater whose sizing rationale was entirely absent. Answering correctly required real thermodynamic computation — not a handbook estimate.

What We Did

Real computation. 12 scenarios. Branded PDF in under 90 minutes.

Scenario matrix

We computed 12 JT scenarios across a full parameter matrix:

  • 2 gas compositions — sweet natural gas + rich associated gas
  • 3 source pressures — 14, 27, and 41 barg
  • 2 cooler outlet temperatures — 25°C and 40°C ambient cases

Tools used

  • CoolProp with Peng-Robinson EOS — μ_JT computed at each letdown stage
  • Python / ReportLab — automated tabular output + phase envelope plots
  • Matplotlib — P-T phase envelope with two-stage letdown trajectories
  • KU Automation branded PDF template — assumptions appendix + comment register

Key technical finding

The richer associated gas composition cooled more than sweet gas at higher source pressures — counterintuitive at first glance, but consistent with CoolProp/PR EOS. The latent-heat moderation effect (which reduces JT cooling for rich gas) only kicks in very close to the phase boundary; at our operating conditions, the heavier C3+ components simply increased μ_JT. This distinction mattered for the EH-203 trim-heater sizing question: the GATTI proposal listed the heater as "optional if required," which we identified as an inadequately justified position for the 41 barg case.

What Worked

Three things that made the difference.

What Comes Next

The scoping study was the opening move.

The JT scoping study positions KU Automation for detailed design engagement if the package is awarded. The next step is a DWSim simulation once three data points are confirmed from the client's side:

  • Actual separator discharge pressure — normal, minimum, and maximum operating range
  • Gauge vs. absolute basis confirmed for all pressure data in the original proposal
  • PCV intermediate setpoint firmed up — determines whether EH-203 trim-heater is required or optional

Once those are confirmed, the JT profile resolves to a single operating point and the EH-203 sizing decision becomes deterministic.
