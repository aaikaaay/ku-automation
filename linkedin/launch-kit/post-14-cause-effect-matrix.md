## 📋 POST BODY (copy this verbatim)

> A Cause & Effect Matrix has exactly one job.
>
> Make sure the right thing happens when the wrong thing occurs.
>
> Every row is a contract between a field instrument and a safety response. High-high level on LT-101 → trip ESD valve XV-203A. Every time. No exceptions.
>
> The problem: C&E matrices grow to 1,000+ rows on complex plants. Manual review catches obvious errors. It misses the systemic ones.
>
> We built an AI C&E reviewer into the KU Automation portal — and what it found in the first three EPC projects surprised us.
>
> **The pattern we kept seeing:**
> ✅ Individual rows reviewed correctly
> ❌ Cross-tag logic conflicts between related causes — completely missed
>
> Instrument A trips Valve B. Instrument C also trips Valve B — with opposing logic on the same ESD event.
>
> Two individually correct rows. One dangerous conflict. Neither visible in a row-by-row scan.
>
> AI doesn't scan rows in sequence. It builds a logic graph of the entire matrix, then runs consistency checks across the whole system:
>
> 🔹 Cross-tag conflict detection (same effect, conflicting causes)
> 🔹 Missing shutdown links vs. HAZOP action items
> 🔹 Instrument tag naming mismatches (C&E vs. P&ID)
> 🔹 Duplicate causes without confirmed shared-effect intent
> 🔹 ESD tier classification inconsistencies (SIL vs. non-SIL logic mixed)
>
> On one 820-row C&E, the AI flagged 23 cross-logic conflicts in 4 minutes. The manual review team had been working on the same document for 2 weeks.
>
