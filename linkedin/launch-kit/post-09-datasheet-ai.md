## 📋 POST BODY (copy this verbatim)

> A lead mechanical engineer on a gas compression project told me he spent 11 days reviewing datasheets for a single compressor package.
>
> 11 days. Not designing anything. Not solving problems. Not mentoring his team. Reviewing a document the vendor had already reviewed, against a spec that already existed, for deviations that could be checked systematically.
>
> Then the vendor came back with Rev B.
>
> The 11 days restarted.
>
> This is the hidden cost that never shows up in project budgets. Engineering hours are tracked against deliverables — P&IDs, calculations, datasheets issued for procurement. But the hours swallowed by vendor datasheet review? They disappear into "engineering support" and "procurement assistance."
>
> Here's what manual datasheet review actually costs on a real project:
>
> 📌 **Senior engineer time** — 2–8 hours per complex datasheet (compressors, heat exchangers, fired equipment). 3,000 datasheets × 3 hours average = 9,000 engineering hours. On a £120/hr blended rate, that's £1.08M in review time alone — before a single revision cycle.
>
> 📌 **Revision spirals** — every Rev B or Rev C means the review restarts. On contested equipment, engineers run 4–5 full reviews of the same document. The vendor "notes" the late comment but makes no change. The deviation follows the equipment to site.
>
> 📌 **Inconsistency** — engineer A checks a pump datasheet on Monday. Engineer B checks an identical pump on Thursday with different reference documents open. The comment registers come out different. The vendor disputes one set. Nobody can explain why.
>
> 📌 **Reviewer bottleneck** — only 3 engineers on the team are qualified to review fired heater or rotating equipment datasheets. Everything queues behind them. The procurement schedule slips while the queue clears.
>
> What AI changes here:
>
> ✅ **Automated spec-vs-datasheet comparison** — AI ingests the project equipment spec, the applicable code (API 610, API 617, ASME VIII, TEMA), and the vendor datasheet simultaneously. It flags every deviation, cites the exact clause, and generates a structured comment register in 8–12 minutes. Not 8 hours.
>
> ✅ **Consistent first-pass reviews** — the same spec is applied every time, on every document, across every reviewer. No variation between engineers, no missed clauses, no reference document mismatches.
>
> ✅ **Smart revision comparison** — when Rev B arrives, AI runs a structured diff against Rev A and flags only the changed fields. Engineers review the delta, not the entire document again.
>
> ✅ **Comment register quality** — AI generates comments in the correct transmittal format (Hold / Comment / For Information), with the exact clause reference and the required vendor action. Junior engineers can close the loop without senior sign-off on every line.
>
> ✅ **Discipline-wide coverage** — process datasheets, instrument datasheets, electrical equipment, mechanical packages, civil foundation loads. One system, consistent review logic across all disciplines.
>
> The senior mechanical engineer's job becomes approving the AI's findings, raising the technically complex Holds, and managing the vendor relationship.
>
> Not running manual cross-checks line by line against a spec they wrote three years ago.
>
> That's what engineering looks like when you stop using £120/hr engineers as document checkers.
