## 📋 POST BODY (copy this verbatim)

> Engineering companies don't lose bids on price.
>
> They lose them on speed, compliance gaps, and pricing errors caught too late.
>
> Last year, a mid-sized EPC firm lost three major tenders in a row. Their technical solution was better. Their pricing was competitive. They lost because by the time their team finished reading and extracting requirements from the 400-page tender packages, their competitor — AI-equipped — had already submitted a polished proposal.
>
> This isn't an isolated case. It's becoming the norm.
>
> **The three bid killers we see repeatedly:**
>
> **① Document processing bottlenecks**
>
> A typical EPC tender package runs 200–500 pages across specifications, drawings, schedules, and commercial terms. Your senior engineers — the expensive ones — spend 40+ hours just reading and extracting requirements before any actual bid work begins.
>
> The AI fix: requirements extracted in under 2 hours. Structured matrix, scope gaps flagged, ambiguities surfaced — ready for the team to actually engineer a response.
>
> One client reduced their initial tender review from 5 days to 4 hours. That's 36 additional hours for solution development and pricing optimization. The same hours their competitor was already spending on the proposal itself.
>
> **② Pricing errors discovered too late**
>
> Every bid team has experienced the Excel nightmare — a margin that looked right on paper that didn't account for material escalation, or a steel tonnage calculation that didn't match the structural drawings, found after submission.
>
> An AI-assisted pricing check caught a £2.3M error on a hull construction bid before it went out. That single catch paid for three years of their AI investment.
>
> **③ Compliance gaps that disqualify on technicalities**
>
> Modern tenders carry extensive compliance requirements — HSE policies, quality certifications, local content obligations, sustainability commitments. Missing even one can eliminate your bid entirely before anyone reads the technical response.
>
> Automated compliance checking mapped one offshore services firm's requirements gap from a 78% to a 96% compliance score. They stopped being eliminated on technicalities.
>
> **The ROI conversation:**
>
> The engineering firms making this shift aren't seeing marginal gains. Across the companies we've worked with:
>
> — Bids submitted per month: 4 → 7
> — Average response time: 18 days → 9 days
> — Win rate: 22% → 31%
> — Cost per bid: £45k → £28k
> — Annual contract value won: £12M → £19M
>
> That last number is the one that gets board attention. +58% annual contract value — not from hiring more engineers, but from removing the bottlenecks that were slowing existing engineers down.
>
> The question isn't whether AI will transform engineering procurement.
>
> It already has. The question is which side of that gap you're on.
