> A FEED contractor we spoke to last quarter had a problem.

> Here's what ISO 42001 actually requires — and why it matters specifically to engineering companies using AI for design review, HAZOP, and document control.
>
> **What ISO 42001 is:**
> ISO/IEC 42001:2023 is the international standard for AI management systems. Think ISO 9001, but for how you govern AI across your organisation — risk, transparency, human oversight, and responsible use.
>
> It's not theoretical. Clients in Abu Dhabi, Saudi, and across the EU are already writing it into tender evaluation criteria.
>
> **The seven clauses that bite engineering firms hardest:**
>
> ①  **AI Risk Assessment** — Every AI tool you deploy must have a documented risk profile. "We tested it and it seemed fine" is not a risk assessment.
>
> ②  **Human Oversight Controls** — The standard requires demonstrable checkpoints where a qualified human reviews AI output before it's acted on. For engineering reviews, this means the AI findings cannot directly generate deliverables without engineer sign-off.
>
> ③  **Data Governance** — What data trained the model? What data is it querying? If you're running a RAG system against your project document store, those data lineage records need to exist.
>
> ④  **Model Transparency** — Can you explain *why* the AI flagged a finding? "The model said so" fails clause 6.1.2. You need traceability to source documents and code references.
>
> ⑤  **Bias & Reliability Testing** — Have you validated the AI's performance across different document types, disciplines, and project complexities? Spot-checking one report is not a validation programme.
>
> ⑥  **Change Management** — When the model is updated, retrained, or swapped out, what's your re-validation process? This catches firms using third-party AI tools with opaque update cycles.
>
> ⑦  **Incident Reporting** — If the AI misses a critical finding that makes it into construction, you need a documented process for capturing, escalating, and learning from it.
>
> **What we built for:**
> KU Automation's reviewer portal was designed around these requirements from the start — not retrofitted. Every finding cites the clause it references (ASME B31.3, API 521, ADNOC specs). Every review goes through an engineer-in-the-loop gate. Every report includes a traceability section showing exactly which document pages the AI queried.
