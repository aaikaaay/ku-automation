## 📋 POST BODY (copy this verbatim)

> A senior process engineer retired from an EPC firm after 31 years.
>
> In those 31 years, he'd solved the same problems — ADNOC material deviation permits, sour service specification challenges, tie-in planning on live processing units, vendor document disputes, hydraulic model calibration — probably 200 times over.
>
> When he left, the firm's answer to every one of those problems walked out the door with him.
>
> The team that replaced him spent six months re-learning things he'd already solved in 2009, 2013, and 2019. The answers were in the project files. But which project? Which revision? Which email thread from which contractor?
>
> That's not a talent problem. That's a knowledge architecture problem — and AI fixes it at the root.
>
> **What institutional knowledge actually looks like in engineering firms:**
>
> After 10–30 years, the average EPC or owner-operator is sitting on:
>
> — Thousands of approved-for-construction documents across dozens of projects
> — Hundreds of vendor communications and dispute resolutions
> — RFIs, technical queries, and non-conformance reports going back decades
> — Design deviation permits with full engineering justifications
> — Lessons-learned registers that haven't been opened since the project closed
> — Calculation packages that justify decisions no one on the current team knows were made
>
> All of it is technically accessible. None of it is practically retrievable.
>
> A junior engineer asking "how did we handle the sour service material spec on the last offshore project?" is not going to find the answer in a SharePoint search that returns 4,800 results.
>
> **What an AI Engineering Knowledge Base delivers:**
>
> The system ingests your project archive — drawings, specs, calculations, RFIs, technical queries, lessons-learned registers, vendor communications — and makes it conversationally searchable:
>
> → "What material spec did we use for sour service piping above 3% H₂S on our last ADNOC project, and who approved the deviation?"
> → "Find all RFI responses where the client accepted a piping flexibility waiver under B31.3."
> → "What was the engineering basis for the PSV sizing on the 2021 Ruwais compressor project?"
> → "Show me every deviation permit we've issued for insulation specification on offshore modules."
>
> Each answer comes with the source document, the revision number, the project reference, and the engineer who signed off. Not a guess — the actual record.
>
> **The compounding return:**
>
> A junior engineer who can query 30 years of institutional knowledge in natural language is effectively mentored by every senior engineer who ever worked at your firm — including the ones who left.
>
> Onboarding compresses from six months to six weeks. Technical queries that previously escalated to the most senior person in the room get resolved before that person is interrupted. Bid teams stop reinventing methodology from scratch because the previous methodology is findable, citable, and adaptable.
>
> And critically — when the next senior engineer retires, nothing walks out the door.
>
> One engineering director put it plainly: "We've been exporting our competitive advantage every time someone hands in their notice."
>
> That's exactly what an AI knowledge base stops.
>
