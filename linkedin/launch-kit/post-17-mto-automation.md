> Your MTO took three engineers six weeks and came back 8% wrong.
>
> On a $40M procurement package, 8% wrong is $3.2M of materials either missing or over-ordered.
>
> And yet every EPC project in the Gulf still does it the same way.
>
> Material take-off is the backbone of engineering procurement — every pipe fitting, valve, structural bolt, and instrument cable must be counted, spec-matched, and entered into the BOM before a single purchase order goes out. On a mid-sized plant, that's 500+ isometrics, 300+ structural drawings, hundreds of pages of instrumentation loop diagrams.
>
> Manual count. Row by row. Engineer by engineer. Under bid deadline pressure.
>
> We built AI MTO into the KU Automation portal. Here's what the numbers looked like across four EPC projects:
>
> 📊 **Time per 100 isometrics:**
> Manual → 40–50 hours
> AI-assisted → 4–6 hours
>
> 📊 **Quantity error rate:**
> Manual → 5–15%
> AI-assisted → 1–5%
>
> 📊 **Revision handling:**
> Manual → full re-count every revision
> AI-assisted → delta extraction (only changed drawings reprocessed)
>
> The AI doesn't get tired on isometric 400. It doesn't misread a 2" elbow as a 3" elbow at 11 PM on a bid deadline. It cross-references every extracted quantity against the piping class index, line list, and material spec automatically — and flags every inconsistency before the BOM gets locked.
>
> The biggest surprise: **revision handling.** When a project issues Rev C isometrics mid-procurement, manual MTO means re-counting. AI MTO means running only the changed sheets and merging the delta. On one project, a 200-drawing revision cycle that would have taken 80 engineer-hours took 4.
>
> For a mid-size contractor running 500 isometrics/month, that's the difference between $180,000/year in MTO labour and $35,000. Same accuracy. Faster. No deadline heroics.
