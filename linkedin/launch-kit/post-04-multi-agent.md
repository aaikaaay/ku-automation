## 📋 POST BODY (copy this verbatim)

> Five AI engineers walked into a design review.
>
> A piping AI, a process AI, a structural AI, a mechanical AI, and a civil AI.
>
> They caught 11 cross-discipline clashes the single-reviewer scan missed entirely.
>
> Here's what happened.
>
> We ran a multi-agent integrated design review on an offshore module package — the kind of IDR an EPC would normally block two weeks of senior engineer time for. Instead of feeding the drawings to one AI model and asking "what's wrong?", we ran five specialist agents in parallel, each reviewing from its own discipline perspective.
>
> Then the agents reconciled.
>
> **What the single-agent pass missed:**
> ❌ A structural opening that the piping agent had already routed a high-pressure steam line through — flagged by the structural agent against AISC 360 §I3.2
> ❌ A relief valve discharge stack positioned 4.3 m below a cable tray — process had sized it correctly, civil had clearances right, nobody saw the elevation conflict until the structural agent looked at both
> ❌ Tie-in points that piping had marked "TBC" which the mechanical agent cross-referenced against confirmed equipment nozzle ratings from the datasheets — two were undersized before anyone modelled the pipe stress
>
> **What this tells us:**
> Single-discipline AI review is not integrated design review.
> It's a smarter checklist.
>
> The value of an IDR isn't that five engineers are smarter than one. It's that each engineer looks at the same drawing with a different model of failure in their head.
>
> Multi-agent AI does the same thing — except it does it in minutes, cites the clause, and doesn't miss a sheet because someone had a flight to catch.
