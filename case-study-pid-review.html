01 · The Challenge

An IFC deadline. A bandwidth crisis. A sour-service P&ID that had to be right.

The client — a Tier-1 EPC contractor executing a sour-gas gathering facility for a National Oil Company in the Middle East — was approaching an Issued-for-Construction (IFC) milestone under significant schedule pressure. A critical process P&ID covering the sour-gas reception and separation train was due to the NOC for formal acceptance.

The challenge was not one of competence — it was one of capacity. The EPC's principal piping engineers were spread across multiple active deliverables. A formal inter-discipline check (IDC) had been conducted, but the throughput of the QA backlog meant that a deep, standards-referenced review against ASME, API, NACE, and client-specific specifications had not been completed. The document was on a trajectory to be issued — without that safety net.

Sour-gas service is unforgiving. Hydrogen sulphide environments demand correct materials selection, rigorous SIL classification, correct relief and vent routing, and full code compliance across every tagged item. The cost of getting it wrong after IFC — with vendor packages committed, construction mobilised, and materials on order — is measured in weeks of delay and hundreds of thousands of dollars in rework. More importantly, in H₂S service, the cost of getting it wrong on site is measured in lives.

IFC Deadline Pressure

05 · Top 5 Critical Findings

The Findings That Would Have Reached the Client

The following five findings are representative of the critical-severity items captured by the review. All are illustrative of the category and nature — specific tag identifiers are not published.

# Finding Severity Code Reference Corrective Action Required
01
PSV Sizing Basis Inconsistent with API 520 Relief Case
Relief case documented in the process datasheet does not match the orifice area designation shown on the P&ID tag. Tag capacity appears undersized for fire case scenario.
 CRITICAL API 520 Pt.I §3.6; API 521 §5.15 Recalculate required orifice area for governing relief case. Reissue tag data sheet and update P&ID designation before IFC.
02
NACE MR0175 Service Declaration Missing on Wetted Parts
Multiple tagged items in confirmed sour-service streams lack the mandatory NACE MR0175/ISO 15156 material declaration. Wetted alloy suitability cannot be verified without this notation.
 CRITICAL NACE MR0175/ISO 15156-2 §6 Add NACE MR0175 service declaration to all applicable wetted-part tags. Issue updated materials selection record to support P&ID revision.
03
SIL Rating Conflict: SIL-2 on P&ID vs. SIL-3 in SIL Register
Safety loop shown with SIL-2 annotation on the P&ID does not match the SIL-3 target defined in the SIL determination study. This misalignment invalidates the functional safety basis for the loop.
 CRITICAL IEC 61511-1 §9.2.3; §10 Reconcile SIL classification between P&ID and SIL determination record. Confirm with functional safety engineer before document reissue.
04
Line Class Breaks Not Shown at Battery Limits
Pipe specification changes at system battery limit tie-ins are not delineated on the drawing. Absence of class-break notation creates ambiguity for procurement, construction, and inspection.
 MAJOR ASME B31.3 §300.2; project P&ID legend Add class-break symbols and pipe spec callouts at all battery limit tie-in points per project P&ID symbology standard.
05
Vent/Drain Routing Violates ASME B31.3 for Hydrocarbon Service
Drain line from a hydrocarbon-bearing separator is shown routing to an open drain system. ASME B31.3 and project specifications require closed drain collection for Category D or sour-service lines.
 CRITICAL ASME B31.3 §322; API 521 §5.4 Reroute drain connection to closed drain header. Add appropriate isolation and blowdown provisions per project specifications before IFC.

06 · The Recommendation

Recommendation D — REJECT

Every review concludes with a structured recommendation on the standard A–D engineering review ladder. The system does not soften findings — it applies the recommendation the data demands.

The A–D Recommendation Ladder

A
Accept
Document meets all applicable requirements. No comments. Cleared for issue or construction without reservation.
B
Accept with Comments
Minor or administrative comments noted. Document may proceed subject to incorporation of comments at next revision — no re-review required.
C
Revise and Resubmit
Significant comments requiring document revision. May proceed after incorporation; a full re-review is recommended depending on the scope of changes.
D
Reject — Resubmit Before IFC This Review
Document contains critical non-conformances that preclude issue. Must not be issued until all critical findings are resolved and the document has been re-reviewed. Construction must not proceed against this revision.

Why D Was the Correct Call

A Recommendation D is not issued lightly. It means the document, in its submitted state, must not be issued. It demands a full corrective cycle before the document re-enters the review gate.

In this case, the volume and nature of the critical findings — spanning PSV sizing, NACE materials compliance, SIL classification conflicts, and improper drain routing — collectively rendered the P&ID non-issuable. Any one of these categories, in isolation, would have been sufficient for a Recommendation C. Together, they mandated D.

Critically, the recommendation was not made on cumulative quantity alone. The SIL classification conflict between the P&ID and the SIL determination register is a single finding that, in a functional safety context, is independently sufficient to block IFC issue. The wrong SIL on a safety loop means the entire safety lifecycle verification for that function is invalidated.

The system is calibrated to match industry norms. A Recommendation D reflects the same judgment a principal engineer would exercise after reviewing the same document — and no less.
