04 · Critical Findings

Top 5 Critical Findings

Of the 18 findings raised, five were classified CRITICAL — any one of which, if missed before IFC, could have triggered a costly post-construction rework cycle or vendor re-submission.

F1
CRITICAL API 610 §6.1.9

NPSH Margin Insufficient — 0.3 m Shown vs. 1.0 m Minimum Required

The datasheet shows a Net Positive Suction Head (NPSH) margin of 0.3 m between NPSHa and NPSHr. API 610 (12th Edition, §6.1.9) mandates a minimum margin of ≥1.0 m for hydrocarbon service at rated conditions. A sub-minimum NPSH margin on a sour-gas charge pump creates a high risk of cavitation, reduced pump life, and potential seal failure in service — with no protection margin in the event of any suction-side process variation.

Recommendation: Vendor to revise suction conditions or impeller selection to achieve NPSHa − NPSHr ≥ 1.0 m at rated flow. Process team to confirm suction-side operating envelope.
F2
CRITICAL API 610 §6.5 / NACE MR0175

Materials Class A-8 Still Shown as VTA at IFC Stage

Materials class A-8 (austenitic stainless steel / duplex SS selection for sour service) remains populated as "VTA" (Vendor To Advise) on the IFC-stage datasheet. At the Issued-for-Construction stage, all materials fields must be closed out with confirmed material grades compliant with NACE MR0175 for H₂S-containing service. An open VTA at IFC on a NACE-critical selection constitutes a significant documentation non-conformance and could invalidate the IFC status of the document.

Recommendation: Vendor to confirm and state specific material grade (e.g., UNS S31803, UNS S32205 or equivalent) with explicit NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 hardness compliance data prior to IFC issue.
F3
CRITICAL API 610 §5.1 / API 610 Table 1

Pump Type (OH2/BB1) Still Shown as VTA at IFC Stage

The pump type/configuration field — expected to show the confirmed OH2 or BB1 classification per API 610 Table 1 — remains annotated as "VTA" on the IFC-stage datasheet. The pump type is a fundamental design parameter that drives decisions on nozzle orientation, bearing housing, baseplate configuration, and mechanical seal arrangement. An unresolved pump-type VTA at IFC means that interconnecting piping, foundation design, and seal support system specifications may all be based on an unconfirmed configuration.

Recommendation: Vendor to confirm and state pump type designation (OH2 or BB1) per API 610 Table 1. EPC to verify that nozzle orientation and layout drawings are consistent with the confirmed type before IFC issue.
F4
CRITICAL API 682 §5 / API 610 §7.8

Mechanical Seal Flush Plan Not Confirmed for Sour Service

The mechanical seal arrangement and flush plan for this sour-hydrocarbon service pump is not confirmed on the datasheet. API 610 (§7.8) requires that the seal system be specified in accordance with API 682, which for a Category 1/2 fluid in sour service (H₂S >50 ppm) typically mandates a tandem or double seal arrangement with a pressurised or unpressurised buffer fluid system (Plan 52/53A/53B). An unspecified seal plan on a sour-service pump represents an unacceptable safety gap at IFC stage.

Recommendation: Vendor and EPC process engineer to confirm API 682 seal category, arrangement, and flush plan designation. Seal support system P&ID reference to be included on the datasheet before IFC.
F5
CRITICAL NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 §7

No NACE MR0175 Hardness Limit Stated for Pressure-Containing Components

The datasheet does not state the maximum hardness limits required by NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 for pressure-containing and wetted metallic components in sour (H₂S-bearing) service. NACE MR0175 specifies maximum hardness values (e.g., 22 HRC / 237 HBW for most carbon steels) that must be met to prevent sulfide stress cracking (SSC) in the presence of hydrogen sulfide. Omitting this requirement from the datasheet means there is no contractual obligation on the vendor to verify or certify compliance.

Recommendation: Add explicit NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156 hardness requirements for all wetted and pressure-containing components to the datasheet. Vendor to provide mill certificates confirming hardness compliance before shipment.

What Could Have Happened Without the Review

An NPSH margin of 0.3 m (vs. API minimum of 1.0 m) would have led to chronic cavitation on the charge pump in hydrocarbon service — accelerated seal wear, unplanned shutdowns, and significant maintenance spend.
Open VTA fields (materials class, pump type) at IFC would have created downstream ambiguity in foundation design, piping layout, and seal support system specification — all potentially requiring redesign once the vendor closed out the VTAs post-procurement.
Missing NACE MR0175 hardness requirements on a sour-service pump without HSE or regulatory contractual backing could have constituted a non-conformance to NACE MR0175 / ISO 15156, with potential safety and insurance implications.
On a fast-track sour-gas project, a single major vendor re-submission cycle after IFC typically costs 4–6 weeks of schedule impact and significant engineer-hour expenditure across the EPC, owner, and vendor teams.
